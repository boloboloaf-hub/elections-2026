The 2026 Midterms, in context

National overview · Election day: November 3, 2026

A historical guide to U.S. midterm elections, with a transparent illustrative 2026 scenario.

Midterms are a national referendum with 435 local House contests, a smaller but consequential Senate map, and turnout that tends to lag presidential years. This edition pairs historical House results with an illustrative 2026 scenario: modeled figures are not reported polls, fundraising disclosures, or election returns.

The midterm pattern

The president’s party has usually lost House seats at the first election between presidential contests. The exceptions matter: national conditions, candidate quality, district boundaries, and the particular seats at stake can all overwhelm the average. How to read this edition

The historical chart is retrospective; the battleground comparisons and turnout page are planning scenarios, not a forecast or a statement of certified 2026 outcomes. A projected margin is measured in percentage points, with positive values favoring the Democratic candidate in the scenario. Replace the scenario inputs with linked, dated sources before using them as current reporting.

Note

A note on uncertainty. A generic margin-of-error band is not a substitute for a poll’s sample design, field dates, likely-voter model, or aggregation method. Scenario inputs on this site are labeled as such and should not be read as polling.
