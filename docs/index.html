Chamber balance: 2024 to 2026

The 119th Congress convened with narrow Republican control. For the 120th Congress, CBS News projected a Democratic House range of 254–267 seats on Nov. 11 and later projected a 56–44 Democratic Senate after Alaska was called. The House bars show the midpoint of the reported range for visual comparison only; whiskers show its full range.

The 119th Congress composition reflects the post-2024 election starting balance (House: 215 Democrats, 220 Republicans; Senate: 47 Democratic caucus members, 53 Republicans). For 2026, the House range is CBS News’ Nov. 11 estimate and the Senate balance is CBS News’ Nov. 17 projection. Sources: CBS House and Senate overview, CBS Alaska Senate result. The 2026 House non-Democratic range is the mathematical remainder from 435; it is not labeled as an all-Republican count.

The president’s party and House losses

The bars show House seat change for the president’s party at each midterm. The 2026 result is intentionally not plotted as an exact seat swing: available reporting confirms Democratic control but gives a range, not a final certified count.

Historical seat changes are the net change in House seats for the president’s party at midterm elections, compiled from official election results. 2018 is shown as a 41-seat Republican loss, consistent with the final House net change. The 2026 annotation is not a numeric result. Source context: Office of the Historian, U.S. House.

Selected 2026 results

These are projected outcomes reported by CBS News, not a substitute for state-certified canvasses. Percentages are included only where the cited source reported a dated figure; other vote totals are left out rather than inferred.

The 2026 Senate result differs substantially from the earlier scenario numbers sometimes circulated online: CBS reported Democratic wins in Maine, North Carolina, Michigan, Ohio, Georgia, New Hampshire and Texas, while Republicans held Florida. See the CBS results overview and Senate results page. Pennsylvania’s Josh Shapiro is included as a governor’s race; Abigail Spanberger’s Virginia victory was in 2025, not a 2026 midterm race.
