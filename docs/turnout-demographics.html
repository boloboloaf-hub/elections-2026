Turnout and demographic shifts

What 2026 sources can—and cannot—tell us

Verified turnout context and demographic comparisons from the 2026 midterms.

The 2026 midterms had the second-highest turnout rate of any midterm year since 1914, behind 2018, according to political scientist Michael McDonald as cited by the Brennan Center on Nov. 6. A final national rate and a complete set of voter-file records were not available in that report. The demographic comparisons below therefore use only published, clearly defined measures.

National turnout trajectory

The chart uses the U.S. Elections Project’s voting-eligible-population (VEP) estimates for 2018 and 2022. The 2026 value is not plotted because a comparable final rate was not available in the cited Nov. 6 reporting; its ranking is stated separately above.

Historical rates: U.S. Elections Project, VEP turnout estimates for 2018 and 2022. 2026 ranking: Brennan Center for Justice, Nov. 6, 2026. The ranking does not supply an exact national percentage.

Latino voters: presidential vote and House vote

CBS News exit-poll graphics reported a larger Democratic share among Latino voters in 2026 than in 2024. The comparison is not like-for-like: 2024 is the presidential vote and 2026 is the House vote. Exit polls are estimates, not certified vote counts.

Percentages read from the CBS News national exit-poll graphic for Latino voters. Shares are rounded; omitted percentage points reflect other candidates and rounding.

Georgia voter-file participation, by race

Preliminary Georgia voter-file records showed a narrower Black–white turnout gap in 2026 than in any midterm election in the prior 20 years. Brennan Center reported two related cohort measures: the share of 2024 voters who did not return in 2026, and the share of 2026 voters who had also voted in 2024. They have different denominators, shown in separate panels.

Source: Brennan Center for Justice, Nov. 6, 2026. These are Georgia registered-voter-file cohort measures, not national turnout rates or estimates for all eligible residents.

Data not yet comparable

This edition does not plot 2026 national turnout by age, education, or urban/suburban/rural residence, nor does it provide a 2018–2026 demographic-margin series. The sources linked here do not provide a complete, consistently defined set of those measures. Exit polls, voter-file participation, and Census turnout rates describe different populations and should not be combined into a single trend without matching definitions.
