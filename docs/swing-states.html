Battlegrounds: what the polls missed

Selected 2026 Senate contests · results and polling errors

Verified Senate outcomes and dated polling-error estimates for eight battleground states.

This page replaces pre-election scenarios with reported 2026 outcomes. Where public sources do not provide comparable final vote shares or race-level spending figures, those values are omitted rather than estimated.

Polling error by state

Pew Research Center compared the final Democratic-minus-Republican polling average with unofficial vote counts as of Nov. 11, 2026. A positive value means the polls understated the Democratic margin. These are signed polling errors, not the candidates’ actual vote margins or poll averages.

Source: Pew Research Center, “A First Look at Polling Performance in 2026”, updated Nov. 12. The results used in its calculations were unofficial counts available Nov. 11; some races were not final.

Political advertising: cycle-level context

Race-level candidate fundraising, independent expenditures, and vote shares are reported through different systems and periods. The comparable total available here is political advertising, not all campaign or outside spending. AdImpact estimates reported by Deadline put 2026 cycle ad spending at $12.06 billion, versus $11.2 billion in 2024. The two-cycle comparison is context, not evidence that spending caused a result.

Source: Deadline, “Political Ad Spending On 2025–26 Cycle Topped $12 Billion”, citing AdImpact. Ad spending is a media-market estimate and is not interchangeable with FEC candidate receipts, disbursements, or independent expenditures.
